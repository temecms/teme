# Scale benchmark — v0.1.0-beta.6

> **Provisional (I-154).** Slice 1.6's release run, on the shared development machine. The million-article data set was seeded again for 1.6h (B-216) with the planted words, the archives' addresses and the twenty-tag post, then brought forward in place for 1.6h's three new indexes (`migrate --database=bench`, 30 s) and vacuumed; this is the second run on it (no reseed).
>
> **The first run stopped the slice** (2026-09-27, its report not kept). Every public page read all 802,202 addresses, since a listing's join compared `char` with `varchar` (B-307); search ranked every match of a common word (70,125 for `tidewater`, 2,781 ms); the front page walked every published post for pinned ones (B-308); an author's archive sorted all their posts (B-309); related posts asked a query per term (B-310); and `bulk.edit`'s statements didn't match the plan's table (B-311). Fixed before this run, each with a plan test: the join compared as text, a partial index for pinned posts written literally in the query, a published-order index for author archives, related posts in one query, Bulk Edit's saves using the person it keeps, search ranking only the newest 1,000 matches (I-458, B-312), a date archive's count stopped at its deepest page (B-316), and no prerendering of addresses with a query string (B-315).
>
> **Every plan rule passes, but one pair accepted under #30** (2026-09-27): check 4's `public: article, 20 tags` ran 23 statements to the random `public: article`'s 25. Every article runs the same 16 or 17 statements whatever its terms, related posts included; what differs from article to article is one caption lookup when the article has a featured image and #30's per-card lookups, each card's author address and image read one at a time (over 40 random articles, 19 to 26 in all). **#29**, a category archive sorting its posts in memory (500 at a million, 2 ms), is accepted for the betas and not flagged on this run: the planner now estimates 29 rows to sort, where it estimated 302.
>
> **Statements against the plan's table** (Task 5): `scheduler.claim` 1,184 (was 1,478: 23 a publication, the scheduler loaded once, and one media lookup for each of the pass's posts with an image, 30 of 50 here); `bulk.edit` 418 a batch of 25, a repeat edit of posts the first run edited (a first edit, measured on 25 untouched posts, is 497; B-311); `editor.checkpoint` 22 (the table's 23 included an image lookup; this run's item has no image), *quick edit, draft waiting* 18, `picker search: river` 1, `editor.compare` 0.
>
> **Time misses with clean plans stay provisional under I-153 and I-154, recorded under B-218's rule (B-322).** The machine was busier during this run: 22.7% average CPU, peaks of 50.7%, where the minute before averaged 12.7%. Scenarios whose code and plans didn't change ran 1.4 to 2.8 times their first-run times (`picker search: river` 538 ms against 323, `palette.mixed` 486 against 323, *title search: lighthouse 5* 324 against 114). Timed just after, on the quiet machine, the public pages' medians were: front page 159 ms, article 96, category 102, author 126, year archive 138, common-word search 240; about two-thirds of each is time in the database, most of it round trips of about 2.5 ms each from Windows to Docker's PostgreSQL (B-218), which #30's per-card lookups multiply. `search.body_rare_word` (the list's In text search for a word 100 items hold, B-199) walks 430,701 rows in the list's order to find a page, a miss that grows with the site (B-321).
>
> **Search (I-458)**, swept on this data (`docs/architecture/search.md`): a rare word and words up to about 10,000 matches go through the full-text index; from about 20,000 the search walks newest first and stops at the cap; no search reads more than about 31,000 rows. A search of many common words together is the one cost that still grows with the site (B-313).
>
